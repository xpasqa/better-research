## Synthesis by Relationship

### X1 Digital Leadership → Z Work Engagement

Evidence is **supportive but mechanism-diverse**. Li et al. (2024) provides direct construct evidence but is cross-sectional; Li, Wang, and Han (2026) strengthens temporal ordering with a two-wave design and identifies digital self-efficacy plus technostress. Decuypere and Schaufeli (2021) provides broader leadership→engagement meta-analytic support but is not Digital Leadership-specific. Therefore, the relationship is theoretically plausible and empirically supported, but not a settled universal causal effect.

### X1 Digital Leadership → Y Individual/Job Performance

Öngel et al. and Sağbaş et al. report positive performance pathways through creativity/innovative behavior. Chatterjee et al. studies Digital Leadership capability at organizational-performance level. Polri-specific Silalahi et al. uses organizational culture as mediator. This cluster shows that **multiple mechanisms already exist**; work engagement cannot be claimed as the only missing mediator.

### X2 Digital Workplace / Digitalization → Z Work Engagement

Zhan and Xie (2025) is especially important because public-sector workplace digitalization exhibits both challenge and threat pathways. Xu et al. (2025) confirms a broader double-edged pattern. Therefore X2 must be operationalized carefully: a positively valenced Digital Workplace can represent enabling conditions, while technostress/overload/fragmentation remain rival or boundary conditions rather than automatically being mixed into the same measurement model.

### X2 Digital Workplace → Y Performance

Chatterjee et al. supports relevance but at organizational level; Selimović et al. links digital workplace transformation with employee performance/well-being in financial services; Xu et al. provides broad meta-analytic evidence. The relationship is credible but outcome-level and construct heterogeneity remain substantial.

### Z Work Engagement → Y Individual Work Performance

This is the **most empirically established relationship** in the model. Corbeanu and Iliescu plus Neuber et al. provide large meta-analytic support. Borst et al. shows sectoral variation, so public-sector effects should not be assumed identical across settings.

### JD-R Mechanism

Bakker and Demerouti (2007, 2017), Mazzetti et al., and Lesener et al. support the motivational process linking resources to engagement and downstream outcomes. However, they do not justify classifying Digital Leadership itself as a job resource by definition, nor do they by themselves establish all direct paths X1→Y and X2→Y.

### Policing / Public-Sector Boundary

Afzal and Panagiotopoulos (2024) and Henderikx and Stoffers (2023) show that digital transformation and leadership in policing are already studied. Silalahi et al. adds Polri-specific DL–performance evidence, albeit with quality caution. Thus the defensible question is **not whether policing has been studied**, but whether the specific integrated explanatory configuration transfers to operational policing under hierarchy, discretion, coordination, readiness, and public-security accountability.

## Contradictory / Mixed Evidence Register

1. Digitalization can be enabling **and** demanding (Zhan & Xie; Xu et al.).
2. Leadership-performance effects often depend on intervening mechanisms (creativity, innovation, culture, self-efficacy, empowerment, commitment).
3. Sector/context changes engagement antecedents/outcomes (Borst et al.).
4. Organizational-performance evidence cannot be substituted for individual work performance (Chatterjee et al.).
5. Polri-specific evidence exists but quality/measurement should be critically appraised (Silalahi et al.).

## Rival Mechanism Register

- digital self-efficacy / digital competence;
- psychological empowerment;
- affective commitment;
- innovative behavior / creativity;
- organizational culture;
- challenge/threat appraisal;
- digital literacy;
- technostress / anxiety / overload;
- system integration/usability;
- discretion and sense of control in policing.

## Contextual Sources — Not Causal/Theoretical Evidence

Official/regulatory sources (Perpres 54/2022, institutional NCSA publication, Humas Polri 2026, LKIP Pasukan Gegana 2025) are used to establish **institutional context and empirical setting only**. They must not be used to infer causal effects among X1/X2/Z/Y.

## Quality Rule for Bab I vs Bab II

- **Bab I:** use only high-value sources necessary for problem architecture, construct relevance, concise mechanism, and preliminary tension.
- **Bab II:** carry full relationship synthesis, measurement evidence, rival mechanisms, direct-path justification, and publication-quality appraisal.
- **Research Gap:** derive only from cross-cluster synthesis and falsification in #17, not from isolated future-research statements.
