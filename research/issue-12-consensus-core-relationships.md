## Relationship Synthesis

### 1. Digital Leadership → Work Engagement

**What is relatively consistent:**

- Digital leadership is positively associated with work engagement in multiple recent studies.
- Mechanisms already tested include empowerment, affective commitment and digital self-efficacy.
- Boundary conditions include emotional intelligence and technostress.

**What is not established:**

- The relationship is not evidence of a universal causal effect. Much of the literature remains cross-sectional.
- Work engagement is not the only plausible mediator; empowerment, self-efficacy, commitment, meaningfulness and job crafting are competing/adjacent mechanisms.

**Implication for dissertation:**

X1→Z is empirically plausible, but the dissertation cannot claim this pathway is novel simply because it is tested in Korbrimob.

### 2. Digital Leadership → Individual/Job Performance

**What is supported:**

- DL–performance relationships have already been studied.
- Existing studies frequently show mediated rather than purely direct relationships, e.g. through creativity or innovative behavior.
- Polri-specific DL→performance studies also exist, although quality varies.

**Key qualification:**

The direct path X1→Y needs its own theoretical justification. JD-R can explain an engagement-mediated process, but should not be stretched to explain every direct path by default.

### 3. Digital Workplace / Workplace Digitalization → Work Engagement

**Evidence is mixed by mechanism, not simply inconsistent:**

- Zhan & Xie show that digitalization can be appraised as a challenge or threat, producing opposite implications for engagement.
- Xu et al.’s meta-analysis supports a broader double-edged-sword interpretation of digital technology at work.

**Implication:**

X2 must be defined carefully. If the instrument captures a positively valenced “quality of digital workplace,” it is conceptually defensible to treat those characteristics as resources. But broader workplace digitalization also generates demands (technostress, anxiety, overload, reduced control). The dissertation should not define X2 itself as simultaneously positive and negative unless its measurement model supports that.

### 4. Digital Workplace / Digital Technology → Performance

**What is supported:**

- Digital workplace/technology is associated with performance in several contexts.
- Chatterjee et al. show an integrated digital-workplace/digital-leadership model linked with organizational performance.
- The meta-analytic digital-technology literature supports positive task-performance effects alongside negative psychological outcomes.

**Qualification:**

Organizational performance, perceived system performance and individual work performance are different outcomes. They cannot be treated as interchangeable evidence for Y.

### 5. Work Engagement → Performance

This is the strongest part of the model empirically.

- Corbeanu & Iliescu report an overall meta-analytic association around r=.37 with job performance.
- Neuber et al. report a stronger association with task performance (ρ≈.483) and also examine longitudinal relations.
- Public-sector meta-analysis shows engagement is relevant across sectors but sectoral differences exist.

**Implication:**

Z→Y is defensible as an association/motivational pathway. It is still incorrect to state that engagement “causes” performance without design-specific qualification.

### 6. JD-R in Digital Work

**Supported core:**

- Resources at multiple levels predict engagement, including leader-level and organizational-level resources.
- The motivational process is well supported by meta-analytic evidence.

**Digital-work complication:**

- Digital conditions may provide resources and simultaneously introduce demands.
- Technostress, digital fatigue, threat appraisal, reduced discretion and system fragmentation are plausible competing pathways.

**Theoretical implication:**

Digital Leadership is better positioned as an **upstream resource-shaping organizational/social condition**, not mechanically as a single job resource. Digital Workplace should be represented through the specific positive work-enabling characteristics actually measured.

### 7. Policing/Public-Sector Boundary

Evidence from policing and public administration shows that transfer from generic employee studies cannot be assumed mechanically:

- police digitalization involves discretion, sense of control and adaptation;
- public-sector workplace digitalization can generate challenge/threat responses;
- police leadership in digital transformation includes people-oriented and change-management requirements;
- engagement–outcome relations can differ by sector.

This supports treating Korbrimob as a **substantive boundary context**, but not as proof that the model will necessarily behave differently.

## Mixed / Contradictory Evidence Register

| Topic | Mixed Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Digitalization → engagement | Positive resource route and negative threat/strain route | “Digital workplace is beneficial” is too broad |
| DL → performance | Some studies show mediation/full mediation rather than simple direct effect | Direct path may be conditional/mechanism-dependent |
| Digital technology → employee outcomes | Meta-analysis finds both positive and negative outcomes | Double-edged rather than uniformly positive |
| Public vs private engagement outcomes | Sector differences exist | Boundary context matters |
| Polri evidence | Local studies report positive DL effects, but quality varies | Must acknowledge without over-weighting low-quality evidence |

## Alternative Mechanisms Register

The following mechanisms already appear in the literature and should be treated as rival/adjacent explanations rather than ignored:

- psychological empowerment;
- affective commitment;
- digital self-efficacy;
- individual creativity;
- innovative behavior;
- job crafting;
- digital literacy;
- technostress/digital fatigue;
- challenge/threat appraisal;
- discretion/sense of control;
- organizational culture;
- system usability/integration.

These variables are **not automatically added** to the locked model; they are used to calibrate theoretical claims and limitations.

## Boundary-Condition Register

- sector: public vs private;
- policing/law-enforcement setting;
- hierarchy and discretion;
- high-intensity operational demands/readiness;
- degree of system integration/usability;
- digital literacy/competence;
- technostress/digital fatigue;
- job/organizational resource configuration;
- outcome level: individual vs unit vs organization.

## Preliminary Research-Gap Implication — Not Final Gap

Evidence does **not** support these gap claims:

- “Digital Leadership → Engagement has not been studied.”
- “Digital Leadership → Performance has not been studied.”
- “Digital Workplace and Digital Leadership have never been combined.”
- “Polri has no Digital Leadership research.”

A more defensible direction for later falsification (#17) is:

> the literature is fragmented across different digital-work constructs, mechanisms, outcome levels and organizational contexts; therefore the unresolved issue is not whether individual relationships exist, but how leadership-related and workplace-related digital conditions are jointly translated into individual work performance through a motivational mechanism, and whether that configuration remains applicable in operational policing.

This is a **candidate explanatory/configuration/boundary gap**, not a final gap claim.
